Lecture 1 · Lie groups and their tangent algebras

Summary and reading

A Lie group has smooth multiplication and inversion. Its tangent space at the identity is a real Lie algebra. Left translation determines the bracket. For embedded matrix groups, the bracket is the matrix commutator. The algebra records local information. Distinct groups can have the same tangent algebra.

Read Hall, second edition, Sections 1.1–1.5 and 3.3–3.6, and Bröcker–tom Dieck, Chapter I, Sections 1–2. The regular level-set theorem and the embedded-submanifold properties are the geometric imports, cited from Warner, Chapter 1. Lecture 2 develops the exponential map and connects these tangent vectors to one-parameter subgroups.

Lecture 2 · Exponential maps and local group structure

Reading

Read Hall, second edition, Chapter 2 on the matrix exponential, Chapter 3 on matrix Lie algebras and homomorphisms, and Sections 5.3–5.7 on BCH and group versus algebra homomorphisms. Kirillov Jr., Chapter 3, treats the general exponential map and the Campbell–Hausdorff formula. Warner, Chapter 3, Sections 3.46–3.47, treats the adjoint representation on a general Lie group. Lecture 3 uses the exponential to study subgroups and quotients.

Lecture 3 · Subgroups, quotients and homogeneous spaces

Summary and reading

A closed subgroup of a Lie group is an embedded subgroup. A real Lie subalgebra determines a connected immersed subgroup, which need not be closed or embedded. For example, the irrational line is dense and not embedded. For closed HHH, the coset space G/HG/HG/H is a manifold whose tangent space at eHeHeH is isomorphic to g/h\g/\mathfrak hg/h. If a Lie group GGG acts smoothly and transitively on a manifold MMM, then MMM is equivariantly diffeomorphic to G/GpG/G_pG/Gp​ for each p∈Mp\in Mp∈M. Normality is a further condition, needed for G/HG/HG/H to be a group. The sphere is diffeomorphic to both SO⁡(3)/SO⁡(2)\SO(3)/\SO(2)SO(3)/SO(2) and SU⁡(2)/U⁡(1)\SU(2)/\UU(1)SU(2)/U(1). The two models have different isotropy actions.

Read Bröcker–tom Dieck, Chapter I, Section 4; Hall, Section 5.9; and Kirillov Jr., Chapter 2. Warner, Chapter 3, is the reference for the cited closed-subgroup, correspondence, quotient and homogeneous-space theorems. Lecture 4 studies the element −I-I−I of SU⁡(2)\SU(2)SU(2) through covering groups.
