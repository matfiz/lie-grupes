Lecture 10

Peter–Weyl and completeness

Lecture 5 proved that matrix coefficients of inequivalent irreducible representations are orthogonal. This lecture proves that finite-dimensional matrix coefficients span a dense subspace of the square-integrable functions on a compact Lie group.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Average a rank-one projection to obtain a nonzero positive compact intertwiner.
  • Prove that the finite-dimensional invariant subspaces span a dense subspace in every strongly continuous unitary representation of a compact Lie group.
  • Identify the finite-dimensional subspaces of L2(K)L^2(K)L2(K) invariant under the right translations ρ\rhoρ with spaces of matrix coefficients, and state the Peter–Weyl theorem with multiplicities.
  • Compute the coefficient blocks of U⁡(1)\UU(1)U(1) and SU⁡(2)\SU(2)SU(2).

Summary

Let KKK be a compact Lie group. In a strongly continuous unitary representation of KKK on H≠0H\neq0H=0, an averaged rank-one projection is a nonzero positive compact intertwiner. Its eigenspaces for nonzero eigenvalues are finite-dimensional and invariant. The finite-dimensional invariant subspaces span a dense subspace. In L2(K)L^2(K)L2(K), smoothing identifies the finite-dimensional ρ\rhoρ-invariant subspaces with spaces of matrix coefficients. Schur orthogonality then gives the Peter–Weyl theorem.
