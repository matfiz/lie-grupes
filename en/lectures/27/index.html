Lecture 27

Compact orbits and geometric quantisation

What this lecture does

A compact coadjoint orbit has a symplectic form. Not every such orbit supplies a representation of the chosen global group. We calculate the obstruction on a sphere. The symplectic area of the sphere divided by 2πℏ2\pi\hbar2πℏ must be integral. An equivariant lift imposes a further condition when the group is SO⁡(3)\SO(3)SO(3). Holomorphic sections then connect the orbit to the representations of the previous lecture.

Definition 27.3. A prequantum line bundle for (M,ω)(M,\omega)(M,ω) at scale ℏ\hbarℏ is a Hermitian line bundle with unitary connection satisfying F∇=−iℏω.\begin{equation} \label{lec27:curvature} F_\nabla=-\frac i\hbar\omega. \end{equation}F∇​=−ℏi​ω.(27.2)

The sign is part of our convention. A prequantum bundle is an initial geometric input. Its space of all smooth sections is usually far larger than the desired representation. A polarisation, such as holomorphicity, supplies a further selection.

We cite the general prequantisation theorem. A closed real 222-form ω\omegaω on a smooth manifold admits a prequantum line bundle at scale ℏ\hbarℏ exactly when its periods divided by 2πℏ2\pi\hbar2πℏ are integers. A period of ω\omegaω is the integral of ω\omegaω over a closed oriented surface mapped smoothly into the manifold, or over an integer combination of such surfaces. Equivalently, [ω/(2πℏ)][\omega/(2\pi\hbar)][ω/(2πℏ)] is an integral cohomology class. See Kirillov, Chapter 1, Section 2.4.

We prove the sphere case directly.

The equivariant lift

Definition 27.5. Let KKK be a Lie group with Lie algebra k\algkk, and let M⊂k∗M\subset\algk^*M⊂k∗ be a coadjoint orbit with its KKS form ω\omegaω. The action must cover the coadjoint action on MMM, be linear and unitary on fibres, preserve the connection, and satisfy the orbit normalisation. This normalisation requires that, at each ℓ∈M\ell\in Mℓ∈M, the stabiliser KℓK_\ellKℓ​ act on the fibre over ℓ\ellℓ with differential X↦iℓ(X)/ℏ,X∈Lie⁡(Kℓ).X\mapsto i\ell(X)/\hbar,\qquad X\in\Lie(K_\ell).X↦iℓ(X)/ℏ,X∈Lie(Kℓ​).

Phase changes and the odd-degree obstruction

One might try to change the SU⁡(2)\SU(2)SU(2) lift by phases so that −I-I−I acts trivially. We show that, for this sphere and its fixed connection, every such phase change is trivial.

Throughout this subsection, an action on O(m)\mathcal O(m)O(m) means a smooth action that is linear and unitary on fibres, as in the definition of an equivariant prequantum bundle. Suppose two such SU⁡(2)\SU(2)SU(2) actions cover the same sphere action and preserve the connection. For fixed ggg, compare their fibre maps. Their ratio is a bundle automorphism covering the identity. It is linear and unitary on each fibre, and each fibre is a complex line. Hence the ratio is multiplication by a smooth function ag:S2→U⁡(1)a_g:S^2\to\UU(1)ag​:S2→U(1). Since both actions preserve the connection, ag−1dag=0.a_g^{-1}da_g=0.ag−1​dag​=0. The sphere is connected, so aga_gag​ is constant. Call this constant χ(g)\chi(g)χ(g). The group-action law then implies χ(gh)=χ(g)χ(h).\chi(gh)=\chi(g)\chi(h).χ(gh)=χ(g)χ(h). Thus changing the lift while preserving the connection can only twist it by a continuous character of SU⁡(2)\SU(2)SU(2).

Every such character is trivial. Its differential is a real Lie algebra map from su(2)\suu(2)su(2) to the abelian algebra iRi\RiR, so it vanishes on brackets. Since [su(2),su(2)]=su(2),[\suu(2),\suu(2)]=\suu(2),[su(2),su(2)]=su(2), the differential vanishes. Since the connected group SU⁡(2)\SU(2)SU(2) is generated by exponentials (Lecture 2), the character is identically one. Consequently a phase change of this kind cannot alter the action of the centre. Hence the natural action of Lecture 26 is the only such SU⁡(2)\SU(2)SU(2) action on O(m)\mathcal O(m)O(m) that covers the sphere action and preserves the connection.

Now suppose that SO⁡(3)\SO(3)SO(3) acted on O(m)\mathcal O(m)O(m) in this way. Composition with the covering SU⁡(2)→SO⁡(3)\SU(2)\to\SO(3)SU(2)→SO(3) would give such an SU⁡(2)\SU(2)SU(2) action on which −I-I−I acts trivially. By uniqueness, that action would be the natural one, where −I-I−I acts on every fibre by (−1)m(-1)^m(−1)m. Hence for odd mmm no SO⁡(3)\SO(3)SO(3) action on O(m)\mathcal O(m)O(m) that is linear and unitary on fibres covers the rotation action and preserves the connection. This conclusion does not use the orbit normalisation.

This is the sign of Lecture 13 on a fibre. The lift to SU⁡(2)\SU(2)SU(2) of the rotation through 2π2\pi2π about the north pole is −I-I−I, and it acts on the fibre by (−1)m(-1)^m(−1)m. For odd mmm, the calculation above shows that no change of phases removes this sign. So SO⁡(3)\SO(3)SO(3) acts only projectively on the sections of odd degree, and its cover SU⁡(2)\SU(2)SU(2) acts as a representation.

27.5 The compact construction and the shift

This section compares the construction with the recipe of Lecture 19. There a subalgebra m\mathfrak mm with ℓ([m,m])=0\ell([\mathfrak m,\mathfrak m])=0ℓ([m,m])=0 gave a character χℓ\chi_\ellχℓ​ of the group of m\mathfrak mm, and we induced from it. In su(2)\suu(2)su(2) this fails for a nonzero ℓ\ellℓ. A real polarisation would have dimension two, and su(2)\suu(2)su(2) has no two-dimensional subalgebra, because the bracket of two independent vectors is perpendicular to their plane. So we pass to the complexified algebra and take the positive Borel subalgebra.

The sphere illustrates the general compact picture. Let KKK be compact, connected, simply connected and semisimple. Let λ\lambdaλ be a dominant integral weight in the real convention dχ=iλd\chi=i\lambdadχ=iλ. Recall the positive Borel subalgebra fixed in Lecture 26. Our sign conventions follow that choice. Self-checks

1. If r=3ℏ/2r=3\hbar/2r=3ℏ/2, find the normalised period and the quantised representation.

Answer. The period divided by 2πℏ2\pi\hbar2πℏ is 2r/ℏ=32r/\hbar=32r/ℏ=3. The bundle is O(3)\mathcal O(3)O(3) and its sections give V3V_3V3​, of dimension four and spin 3/23/23/2.

2. Does that sphere admit an SO⁡(3)\SO(3)SO(3)-equivariant prequantum lift with the orbit normalisation?

Answer. No. Its stabiliser character would be e3it/2e^{3it/2}e3it/2 on a rotation of period 2π2\pi2π. It takes the value −1-1−1 at t=2πt=2\pit=2π.

3. Why can the connection have local potentials although no global potential exists?

Answer. The difference of two local potentials is the logarithmic differential of a transition function. A nonzero curvature period prevents a single global primitive but is consistent with these local potentials.

Summary

The SU⁡(2)\SU(2)SU(2) orbit of radius r>0r>0r>0 has symplectic area 4πr4\pi r4πr. Prequantisation requires m=2r/ℏm=2r/\hbarm=2r/ℏ to be integral.
