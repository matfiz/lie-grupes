Smooth Vectors, KKK-Types and (g,K)(\g,K)(g,K)-Modules

What this lecture does

A representation of a noncompact group usually acts on an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space. We use its derivatives on smooth vectors. This lecture extracts an algebraic object from such a representation. The object consists of the smooth vectors that are finite under a compact subgroup KKK. On these vectors g\gg and KKK act together, subject to compatibility conditions. The model is SL⁡(2,R)\SL(2,\R)SL(2,R) with K=SO⁡(2)K=\SO(2)K=SO(2).

21.1 Smooth vectors and KKK-finite vectors

Throughout, GGG is a Lie group with Lie algebra g\gg. The subgroup K⊆GK\subseteq GK⊆G is compact, with Lie algebra k⊆g\algk\subseteq\gk⊆g. The representation Π\PiΠ of GGG on a Hilbert space H\mathcal HH is strongly continuous. Here each operator Π(g)\Pi(g)Π(g) is bounded, linear and invertible. The map g↦Π(g)vg\mapsto\Pi(g)vg↦Π(g)v is norm continuous for every v∈Hv\in\mathcal Hv∈H. Unitarity is assumed only where stated.

Definition 21.1. A vector v∈Hv\in\mathcal Hv∈H is smooth if the orbit map G→HG\to\mathcal HG→H, g↦Π(g)vg\mapsto\Pi(g)vg↦Π(g)v, is smooth. For unitary representations, the following facts were imported in Lecture 15. We now also import the density and invariance facts (F1)–(F2) for strongly continuous representations by bounded operators. A source for this more general smooth-vector theorem is Knapp RT, Sections III.3–4. Here Knapp RT is A. W. Knapp, Representation Theory of Semisimple Groups: An Overview Based on Examples (2001).

21.2 The compatibility conditions

The g\gg-action extends complex-linearly to gC\g_\CgC​. Both sides of the covariance condition (c) are complex-linear in XXX, so the covariance condition also holds for X∈gCX\in\g_\CX∈gC​.

The derivative condition (b) involves only elements exp⁡(tX)\exp(tX)exp(tX), which lie in the identity component of KKK. Hence, if KKK is disconnected, the KKK-action carries information that k\algkk cannot detect (Exercise 21.7).

21.4 What the (g,K)(\g,K)(g,K)-module does not record

A (g,K)(\g,K)(g,K)-module has no topology. It does not record the norm or the action of elements of GGG outside KKK. We cite theorems that relate it back to representations. None of them is proved or used later in this lecture.

Admissibility. Let GGG be a connected linear reductive group with maximal compact subgroup KKK (Lecture 20). The following two statements are in Knapp RT, Chapter VIII.

  1. If a strongly continuous Hilbert representation Π\PiΠ is admissible and Π∣K\Pi|_KΠ∣K​ is unitary, then every KKK-finite vector is smooth, so HK∞=HK\mathcal H_K^\infty=\mathcal H_KHK∞​=HK​.

  2. (Harish-Chandra) Every irreducible unitary representation of GGG is admissible.

The circle example, Example 21.4, shows the conclusion of the smoothness statement (i) directly in the case G=KG=KG=K.

Globalization. Passing from a (g,K)(\g,K)(g,K)-module to a continuous group representation is called globalization. A Harish-Chandra module is an admissible (g,K)(\g,K)(g,K)-module finitely generated over U(gC)U(\g_\C)U(gC​). A positive definite Hermitian form on a Harish-Chandra module is invariant if the KKK-action is unitary and the g\gg-action is skew-symmetric for real X∈gX\in\gX∈g.

For connected linear reductive GGG with maximal compact KKK, an irreducible Harish-Chandra module with an invariant positive definite Hermitian form has an irreducible unitary globalization. It is unique up to unitary equivalence. This theorem is cited from P. Etingof’s MIT 18.757 notes. The globalization is Theorem 7.5, printed p. 38, and its uniqueness is Proposition 7.1, printed p. 37. The theorem supplies an action of GGG on a Hilbert space completing the module, together with its strong continuity. A completion of the vector space alone supplies neither.

One (g,K)(\g,K)(g,K)-module can arise from completions of different kinds. Take G=K=SO⁡(2)G=K=\SO(2)G=K=SO(2) as in the circle example, Example 21.4. Translation also acts continuously on the Banach space C(R/2πZ)C(\R/2\pi\mathbb{Z})C(R/2πZ) with the supremum norm, which is not a Hilbert space. With the same definitions, the smooth KKK-finite vectors of C(R/2πZ)C(\R/2\pi\mathbb{Z})C(R/2πZ) are again the trigonometric polynomials. So L2(R/2πZ)L^2(\R/2\pi\mathbb{Z})L2(R/2πZ) and C(R/2πZ)C(\R/2\pi\mathbb{Z})C(R/2πZ) have the same (g,K)(\g,K)(g,K)-module.

This common module has infinitely many distinct circle types and is not finitely generated over U(kC)U(\algk_\C)U(kC​). Thus this example is outside the irreducible Harish-Chandra theorem. The more general Casselman–Wallach globalization theorem belongs to further study.

Integration. The identities among dΠ(E)d\Pi(E)dΠ(E), dΠ(F)d\Pi(F)dΠ(F) and dΠ(H′)d\Pi(H')dΠ(H′) were valid because Π\PiΠ was given. Formal commutation relations among unbounded operators do not by themselves produce a group representation. For the modules of Exercise 21.3, the question of which ones come from unitary representations is taken up in Lecture 22 and Lecture 23.

Summary

Let KKK be a compact subgroup of GGG. For a strongly continuous representation of GGG on a Hilbert space, the smooth KKK-finite vectors carry an action of g\gg and of KKK. The derivative condition (b) relates the two actions on k\algkk. The covariance condition (c) describes how the KKK-action transforms the g\gg-action. The proof that g\gg preserves KKK-finiteness uses the covariance condition.

For the circle, the KKK-finite vectors are the trigonometric polynomials. For SL⁡(2,R)\SL(2,\R)SL(2,R) with K=SO⁡(2)K=\SO(2)K=SO(2), the covariance condition (c) shows that EEE and FFF shift the circle KKK-types by 222 and −2-2−2, respectively. The derivative condition (b) shows that H′H'H′ acts on the KKK-type nnn by multiplication by nnn. For this connected KKK, the brackets [H′,E]=2E[H',E]=2E[H′,E]=2E and [H′,F]=−2F[H',F]=-2F[H′,F]=−2F then give the same shifts.

For connected linear reductive GGG with maximal compact subgroup KKK, three results are cited. Irreducible unitary representations are admissible. In an admissible strongly continuous Hilbert representation Π\PiΠ with Π∣K\Pi|_KΠ∣K​ unitary, KKK-finite vectors are smooth. An irreducible Harish-Chandra module with an invariant positive definite Hermitian form has an irreducible unitary globalization, unique up to unitary equivalence.
