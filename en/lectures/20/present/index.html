Lecture 20

Real Forms and Noncompact Structure

A connected real semisimple group with finite centre has a product decomposition into a compact subgroup, an abelian subgroup and a nilpotent subgroup. The orbit theorem of Lecture 19 concerns nilpotent groups and does not apply to such a group, so we start from its structure. This lecture compares compact and split real forms and states the Cartan and Iwasawa decompositions. It proves both decompositions for SL⁡(2,R)\SL(2,\R)SL(2,R), where they give coordinates on the upper half-plane.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Distinguish the compact and split examples of real forms using their Killing forms and Cartan involutions.
  • State the Cartan and Iwasawa decompositions with all their hypotheses.
  • Compute g=kang=kang=kan and g=kexp⁡Xg=k\exp Xg=kexpX for an explicit matrix in SL⁡(2,R)\SL(2,\R)SL(2,R).
  • Read both decompositions as coordinates on the upper half-plane.

20.1 Real forms and Cartan involutions

Throughout this section gC\g_\CgC​ is a complex semisimple Lie algebra with Killing form κ(X,Y)=tr⁡(ad⁡X ad⁡Y).\kappa(X,Y)=\tr(\ad X\,\ad Y).κ(X,Y)=tr(adXadY). We cite Knapp, Lie Groups Beyond an Introduction, digital second edition 2023, as Knapp Beyond.

Definition 20.1. A real form of gC\g_\CgC​ is a real Lie subalgebra g⊂gC\g\subset\g_\Cg⊂gC​ with gC=g⊕ig\g_\C=\g\oplus i\ggC​=g⊕ig as real vector spaces.

A real basis of g\gg is then a complex basis of gC\g_\CgC​. For X∈gX\in\gX∈g, the matrix of ad⁡X\ad XadX in this basis is real. It is the same over R\RR and over C\CC. Hence the Killing form of g\gg is the restriction κ∣g\kappa|_{\g}κ∣g​. It is real and nondegenerate. Its signature is unchanged by real isomorphisms.

Definition 20.2. A real form g\gg is compact if κ∣g\kappa|_{\g}κ∣g​ is negative definite. It is split if it contains an abelian subalgebra h0\mathfrak h_0h0​ with the following two properties. Every ad⁡H\ad HadH with H∈h0H\in\mathfrak h_0H∈h0​ is diagonalisable over R\RR. The sum h0⊕ih0\mathfrak h_0\oplus i\mathfrak h_0h0​⊕ih0​ is a Cartan subalgebra of gC\g_\CgC​.

Every complex semisimple Lie algebra has both types. The Lie Algebras course does not treat real forms, so we cite this fact from Knapp Beyond, Section VI.1 (Theorem 6.6, Corollary 6.10 and Theorem 6.11).

The construction starts from a Chevalley basis. This basis consists of the coroots HαH_\alphaHα​ and of root vectors EαE_\alphaEα​ with [Eα,E−α]=Hα[E_\alpha,E_{-\alpha}]=H_\alpha[Eα​,E−α​]=Hα​. Knapp scales these vectors by real factors, which does not change the real spans below. The real span of the Chevalley basis is a split form, with h0\mathfrak h_0h0​ the real span of the HαH_\alphaHα​. The elements iHαiH_\alphaiHα​, Eα−E−αE_\alpha-E_{-\alpha}Eα​−E−α​ and i(Eα+E−α)i(E_\alpha+E_{-\alpha})i(Eα​+E−α​) span a compact form. For sl(2,C)\sllie(2,\C)sl(2,C), with Eα=EE_\alpha=EEα​=E, E−α=FE_{-\alpha}=FE−α​=F and Hα=HH_\alpha=HHα​=H, these elements are the matrices AAA, BBB and CCC of the next example.

Example 20.3 proves both claims for sl(2,C)\sllie(2,\C)sl(2,C), and Proposition 20.5 proves them for sl(n,C)\sllie(n,\C)sl(n,C). The general case stays cited.

The name “compact” refers to compact groups. By Lecture 6, the Lie algebra of a compact group has an Ad⁡\AdAd-invariant inner product, so each ad⁡X\ad XadX is skew and κ(X,X)=tr⁡((ad⁡X)2)≤0.\kappa(X,X)=\tr\big((\ad X)^2\big)\le 0 .κ(X,X)=tr((adX)2)≤0. 20.2 Restricted roots and the global decompositions

Fix a real semisimple Lie algebra g\gg with Cartan involution θ\thetaθ, and write g=k⊕p\g=\algk\oplus\algpg=k⊕p.

Lemma 20.6. For X∈pX\in\algpX∈p, the operator ad⁡X\ad XadX is symmetric for κθ\kappa_\thetaκθ​.

Proof. Since θ\thetaθ is an automorphism and θX=−X\theta X=-XθX=−X, we have [X,θZ]=−θ[X,Z][X,\theta Z]=-\theta[X,Z][X,θZ]=−θ[X,Z]. Theorem 20.8 (Cartan and Iwasawa decompositions; cited). Let GGG be a connected semisimple Lie group with finite centre. In §20.3 we prove both forms for SL⁡(2,R)\SL(2,\R)SL(2,R), and we also obtain the singular value decomposition G=KAKG=KAKG=KAK.

A real form keeps the finite-dimensional complex modules of gC\g_\CgC​, by restriction and complex-linear extension. It does not keep their unitarity. For example, SL⁡(2,R)\SL(2,\R)SL(2,R) acts on Vm=SymmC2V_m=\mathrm{Sym}^m\C^2Vm​=SymmC2. For m≥1m\ge1m≥1 and t≠0t\ne0t=0 the matrix diag⁡(et,e−t)\diag(e^t,e^{-t})diag(et,e−t) has the eigenvalue emte^{mt}emt on VmV_mVm​, and ∣emt∣≠1|e^{mt}|\neq1∣emt∣=1. So no inner product makes VmV_mVm​ unitary for SL⁡(2,R)\SL(2,\R)SL(2,R), whereas SU⁡(2)\SU(2)SU(2) has one by Lecture 5. To build unitary representations of SL⁡(2,R)\SL(2,\R)SL(2,R) we use infinite-dimensional spaces, with KKK in Lecture 21 and KANKANKAN in Lecture 22.

In physics language, the adjoint action identifies sl(2,R)\sllie(2,\R)sl(2,R) with so(2,1)\soo(2,1)so(2,1). Then k\algkk is the rotation generator and p\algpp consists of the boosts, and [p,p]⊂k[\algp,\algp]\subset\algk[p,p]⊂k says that the commutator of two boosts is a rotation (Lecture 24).

The split form sl(n,R)\sllie(n,\R)sl(n,R) has real rank n−1n-1n−1. For connected semisimple groups with finite centre, the cited Cartan and Iwasawa theorem gives G=Kexp⁡pG=K\exp\algpG=Kexpp and G=KANG=KANG=KAN. For SL⁡(2,R)\SL(2,\R)SL(2,R) we proved both decompositions, with explicit inverses. On H\mathbb HH, identified with G/KG/KG/K, the subgroup NANANA gives Cartesian coordinates. The KAKKAKKAK form describes H\mathbb HH as the disjoint union of the KKK-orbits of the points ie2tie^{2t}ie2t with t≥0t\ge0t≥0.
