Lecture 26

Equivariant line bundles and Borel–Weil

What this lecture does

Induced representations act on equivariant functions. The same functions can be read as sections of a vector bundle. In the Borel–Weil setting, holomorphic sections of an associated line bundle form a finite-dimensional representation. We compute these holomorphic sections completely for the projective line. The general Borel–Weil theorem is a cited extension.

We also need a holomorphic version of this construction. A function on an open subset of Cd\C^dCd is holomorphic if it is real C∞C^\inftyC∞ and its differential is C\CC-linear at every point. Polynomials are holomorphic, and so are quotients of polynomials where the denominator is nonzero. Composition of holomorphic maps is holomorphic by the chain rule.

Let Φ\PhiΦ be a map between open subsets of Cd\C^dCd with holomorphic components and invertible differential at a point. The real inverse function theorem gives a smooth local inverse. Its differential is the inverse of a C\CC-linear map, hence C\CC-linear. So the local inverse is holomorphic. This is the holomorphic inverse function theorem. A complex manifold is a Hausdorff, second-countable topological space covered by charts. These charts are homeomorphisms of open sets onto open subsets of Cd\C^dCd. The transition maps between them have holomorphic components. A map between complex manifolds is holomorphic if it is continuous and its expressions in charts have holomorphic components.

A complex submanifold of a complex manifold MMM is a subset NNN with the following property. Each point of NNN lies in a chart of MMM in which NNN is given by the vanishing of some coordinates. The remaining coordinates give charts of NNN. Hence a holomorphic map into MMM with values in NNN is also holomorphic as a map into NNN. We call this the slice-chart property.

A complex Lie group is a group with a complex manifold structure for which multiplication and inversion are holomorphic. The groups KCK_\CKC​ of Lecture 14 are examples. A closed complex subgroup of a complex Lie group GGG is a closed subgroup that is also a complex submanifold.

26.5 The general Borel–Weil theorem

Let KKK be compact, connected, simply connected and semisimple. Its complexification G=KCG=K_\CG=KC​ was introduced in Lecture 14. Sepanski, Compact Lie Groups, states Borel–Weil for a matrix model of GGG, so we first identify the two groups.

A faithful representation ρ\rhoρ of KKK exists [C: Bröcker and tom Dieck (BD), Section III.4, Theorem 4.1]. Averaging makes ρ\rhoρ unitary (Lecture 5). Summary

Equivariant functions correspond to sections of associated bundles. On CP1\mathbb{CP}^1CP1 and for m≥0m\ge0m≥0, the transition rule for O(m)\mathcal O(m)O(m) identifies holomorphic sections with homogeneous polynomials of degree mmm. Borel–Weil extends this construction to flag manifolds.
