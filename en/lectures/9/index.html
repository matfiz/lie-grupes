Lecture 9

Weyl integration and character calculations

What this lecture does

On a compact connected group, a class function is determined by its values on a maximal torus. This lecture shows that the Haar integral of a class function can also be computed on the torus. The density is a product over root planes. A factor 1/∣W∣1/|W|1/∣W∣ corrects for repeated conjugates. We then use the formula to check character norms and tensor-product multiplicities.

Summary

Let KKK be a compact connected Lie group with maximal torus TTT. Every regular element of KKK has exactly ∣W∣|W|∣W∣ preimages under the conjugation map K/T×T→KK/T\times T\to KK/T×T→K. Relative to the normalised measures, the Jacobian of this map at (gT,t)(gT,t)(gT,t) is det⁡(1−Ad⁡t)\det(1-\Ad_t)det(1−Adt​) on k/t\algk/\algtk/t. This determinant is a product of root-plane factors ∣1−tα∣2|1-t^\alpha|^2∣1−tα∣2 over the positive roots α\alphaα. The Weyl denominator identity fixes the constant. For SU⁡(2)\SU(2)SU(2) and a continuous class function fff, the Haar integral of fff is 2π∫0πf(tθ)sin⁡2θ dθ\frac2\pi\int_0^\pi f(t_\theta)\sin^2\theta\,d\thetaπ2​∫0π​f(tθ​)sin2θdθ. With the character formula in the form (9.7), norms and multiplicities become Fourier calculations on TTT. This form never needs ρ\rhoρ to be a character.
