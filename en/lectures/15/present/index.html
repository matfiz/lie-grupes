Lecture 15

Strong Continuity and Infinitesimal Generators

Part II studies unitary representations on infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces. Their derivatives may exist only on a proper subspace. This lecture fixes the continuity hypothesis, defines the derivative on smooth vectors, and uses Stone’s theorem to identify the skew-adjoint generator of each one-parameter subgroup, together with its domain. Translations on L2(R)L^2(\R)L2(R) are the model throughout.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Prove strong continuity of a homomorphism into the unitary group from continuity at the identity on a dense set.
  • Define smooth vectors and dΠd\PidΠ, and prove that each dΠ(X)d\Pi(X)dΠ(X) is skew-symmetric on smooth vectors.
  • State Stone’s theorem with its hypotheses, and identify the closure of dΠ(X)d\Pi(X)dΠ(X) with the generator.
  • Locate explicit vectors relative to the domains for translations: Schwartz functions, a non-Schwartz vector in the domain, and a vector outside it.

15.1 From matrices to Hilbert spaces

Let Π ⁣:G→GL⁡(V)\Pi\colon G\to\GL(V)Π:G→GL(V) be a continuous representation on a finite-dimensional complex space VVV. Continuous homomorphisms of Lie groups are smooth (Lecture 3). So every X∈gX\in\gX∈g gives an operator dΠ(X)=ddt∣t=0Π(exp⁡tX)∈End⁡(V),Π(exp⁡tX)=et dΠ(X).d\Pi(X)=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\Pi(\exp tX)\in\End(V),\qquad \Pi(\exp tX)=e^{t\,d\Pi(X)}.dΠ(X)=dtd​​t=0​Π(exptX)∈End(V),Π(exptX)=etdΠ(X). For compact GGG, the Peter–Weyl theorem (Lecture 10) decomposes each unitary representation into finite-dimensional subrepresentations. Noncompact groups behave differently. The representations studied in Part II are infinite-dimensional, and the derivative may be defined only on a proper subspace. The basic example is the translation representation on L2(R)L^2(\R)L2(R), so we first construct that space.

15.2 Strong continuity

Definition 15.1. Let H\mathcal HH be a complex Hilbert space and U⁡(H)\UU(\mathcal H)U(H) its group of unitary operators. A unitary representation of a Lie group GGG on H\mathcal HH is a homomorphism Π ⁣:G→U⁡(H)\Pi\colon G\to\UU(\mathcal H)Π:G→U(H) such that the map G→HG\to\mathcal HG→H, g↦Π(g)vg\mapsto\Pi(g)vg↦Π(g)v, is continuous for every v∈Hv\in\mathcal Hv∈H. This condition is strong continuity. The homomorphism is norm continuous if g↦Π(g)g\mapsto\Pi(g)g↦Π(g) is continuous for the operator norm.

Lemma 15.2. Let Π ⁣:G→U⁡(H)\Pi\colon G\to\UU(\mathcal H)Π:G→U(H) be a homomorphism. Let D⊂HD\subset\mathcal HD⊂H be dense, and suppose g↦Π(g)wg\mapsto\Pi(g)wg↦Π(g)w is continuous at eee for every w∈Dw\in Dw∈D. Then Π\PiΠ is strongly continuous.

Proof. Let v∈Hv\in\mathcal Hv∈H and w∈Dw\in Dw∈D. Since Π(g)\Pi(g)Π(g) is unitary, ∥Π(g)v−v∥≤∥Π(g)(v−w)∥+∥Π(g)w−w∥+∥w−v∥=2∥v−w∥+∥Π(g)w−w∥.\|\Pi(g)v-v\|\le\|\Pi(g)(v-w)\|+\|\Pi(g)w-w\|+\|w-v\|=2\|v-w\|+\|\Pi(g)w-w\|.∥Π(g)v−v∥≤∥Π(g)(v−w)∥+∥Π(g)w−w∥+∥w−v∥=2∥v−w∥+∥Π(g)w−w∥. At another point hhh we use ∥Π(g)v−Π(h)v∥=∥Π(h−1g)v−v∥,\|\Pi(g)v-\Pi(h)v\|=\|\Pi(h^{-1}g)v-v\|,∥Π(g)v−Π(h)v∥=∥Π(h−1g)v−v∥, and h−1g→eh^{-1}g\to eh−1g→e as g→hg\to hg→h. 

Proposition 15.3. The translations λ\lambdaλ form a unitary representation of R\RR on L2(R)L^2(\R)L2(R).

Proof. Only strong continuity remains. We apply the dense-set criterion, Lemma 15.2, with D=Cc(R)D=C_c(\R)D=Cc​(R). Let f∈Cc(R)f\in C_c(\R)f∈Cc​(R) vanish outside [−R,R][-R,R][−R,R]. 15.7 Summary

Unitary representations are required to be strongly continuous, not norm continuous. Smooth vectors form a dense invariant subspace H∞\mathcal H^\inftyH∞. On it, dΠd\PidΠ is a representation of g\gg by skew-symmetric operators. Stone’s theorem attaches a skew-adjoint generator to each strongly continuous unitary representation of R\RR. The closure of dΠ(X)d\Pi(X)dΠ(X) on H∞\mathcal H^\inftyH∞ is this generator. For translations, −d/dx-d/dx−d/dx on S(R)\mathcal S(\R)S(R) is skew-symmetric but not skew-adjoint. Its closure has the strictly larger domain D(A)D(A)D(A), and D(A)D(A)D(A) is still not all of L2(R)L^2(\R)L2(R).
