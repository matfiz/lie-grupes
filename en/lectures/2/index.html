Exponential maps and local group structure

What this lecture does

A tangent vector at the identity generates a one-parameter subgroup. The exponential map collects these subgroups into one map from the Lie algebra to the group. We ask how much of the multiplication is determined by the bracket. Near the identity, the bracket determines the whole multiplication. Far from the identity, it does not.

Summary

Every continuous one-parameter subgroup of GL⁡n(C)\GL_n(\C)GLn​(C) is t↦exp⁡(tX)t\mapsto\exp(tX)t↦exp(tX). For a general Lie group, flows of left-invariant fields define exp⁡\expexp, a local diffeomorphism at 000. Homomorphisms intertwine exponentials, and connected groups are generated by exponentials. The identity component is an open and closed normal subgroup with the same Lie algebra. The adjoint representation satisfies Ad⁡(exp⁡X)=ead⁡X\Ad(\exp X)=e^{\ad X}Ad(expX)=eadX and ad⁡X(Y)=[X,Y]\ad X(Y)=[X,Y]adX(Y)=[X,Y], and elements with [X,Y]=0[X,Y]=0[X,Y]=0 satisfy exp⁡(X+Y)=exp⁡Xexp⁡Y\exp(X+Y)=\exp X\exp Yexp(X+Y)=expXexpY. Near the identity, the BCH series computes multiplication from the bracket. Globally, exp⁡\expexp need not be injective or surjective, and the Lie algebra g\gg does not determine the components of GGG.
