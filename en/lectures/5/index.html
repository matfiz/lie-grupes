Haar Measure, Averaging and Orthogonality

What this lecture does

For a finite group GGG, averaging with ∣G∣−1∑g∈G|G|^{-1}\sum_{g\in G}∣G∣−1∑g∈G​ proves unitarisability, complete reducibility and orthogonality of matrix coefficients. In this lecture we build an integral on a compact Lie group that replaces this average. It is linear and positive, has total mass 111, does not change when the integrand is shifted on either side, and is strictly positive on nonzero nonnegative functions. Sections 5.1 and 5.2 construct it. Sections 5.3 and 5.4 use no other property of it, and they record which property each argument uses.

Summary

On a compact Lie group GGG of positive dimension, left translation of one positive density on g\gg gives the Haar integral. Compactness forces ∣det⁡Ad⁡∣=1|\det\Ad|=1∣detAd∣=1, which gives right invariance. A covering argument by translates gives strict positivity. With these properties, the finite-group proofs of unitarisability, complete reducibility and Schur orthogonality carry over with the sum replaced by the integral. By the matrix-entry relations, Corollary 5.13, the matrix entries of an irreducible unitary representation of dimension ddd in an orthonormal basis are pairwise L2L^2L2-orthogonal, and each has squared norm 1/d1/d1/d. Completeness of matrix coefficients is the Peter–Weyl theorem of Lecture 10.
