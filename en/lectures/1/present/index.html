Lecture 1

Lie groups and their tangent algebras

A Lie group has both a group structure and a smooth geometry. We describe that geometry near the identity and recover its Lie algebra. The group SU⁡(2)\SU(2)SU(2) will give an example in which every step can be calculated.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Identify the real dimension of a matrix Lie group.
  • Find tangent spaces by differentiating a defining condition and exhibiting curves.
  • Recover a matrix Lie bracket from left translations.
  • Distinguish a real Lie algebra from its complexification.

The Lie algebras in the earlier course were complex. The tangent algebra of a real Lie group is a real Lie algebra. We will always state the field of scalars.

1.1 Smooth coordinates

A sphere cannot be described by one set of Euclidean coordinates without omitting a point or introducing a singularity. It can be described by several overlapping coordinate systems. A smooth manifold formalises this local description.

A topology specifies which subsets are open. A neighbourhood of a point is a set containing an open set around that point. A topological space is Hausdorff if distinct points have disjoint open neighbourhoods. It is second countable if its topology has a countable basis, namely a countable collection of open sets such that every open set is a union of some of them.

A map between topological spaces is continuous if the inverse image of every open set is open. A homeomorphism is a continuous bijection whose inverse is continuous.

Definition 1.1 (Smooth manifold). A smooth real manifold of dimension ddd is a topological space MMM covered by coordinate charts φ ⁣:U⟶V⊆Rd.\varphi\colon U\longrightarrow V\subseteq\R^d.φ:U⟶V⊆Rd. Each chart has open domain U⊆MU\subseteq MU⊆M and is a homeomorphism onto an open set VVV. Where the domains of two charts φ\varphiφ and ψ\psiψ overlap, the coordinate change ψ∘φ−1\psi\circ\varphi^{-1}ψ∘φ−1 is infinitely differentiable. We require MMM to be Hausdorff and second countable.

A subset of a topological space has the subspace topology: its open sets are its intersections with open sets of the space. For subsets of Euclidean space we use this topology. For example, an open arc is open in the circle.

Subsets of Euclidean space are Hausdorff and second countable, so all the matrix groups in this lecture satisfy both conditions. Lecture 2 uses the Hausdorff condition for uniqueness of solutions of differential equations on a manifold. Lecture 3 uses second countability for homogeneous spaces. A smooth map between manifolds is a continuous map whose coordinate expressions are infinitely differentiable on their open domains. A diffeomorphism is a smooth bijection whose inverse is smooth.

1.2 Smooth groups

Product charts φ×ψ\varphi\times\psiφ×ψ on U×VU\times VU×V give M×NM\times NM×N a smooth manifold structure of dimension dim⁡M+dim⁡N\dim M+\dim NdimM+dimN. A finite product of Hausdorff, second-countable spaces has both properties.

Definition 1.3 (Lie group). A real Lie group is a group GGG that is also a smooth real manifold, with smooth multiplication and inversion: G×G⟶G,(g,h)⟼gh,G⟶G,g⟼g−1.G\times G\longrightarrow G,\quad (g,h)\longmapsto gh, \qquad G\longrightarrow G,\quad g\longmapsto g^{-1}.G×G⟶G,(g,h)⟼gh,G⟶G,g⟼g−1. A Lie group homomorphism is a smooth group homomorphism.

The additive group Rd\R^dRd is a Lie group. Any finite group with the discrete topology is also a Lie group, since each one-point subset is the domain of a chart modelled on R0\R^0R0. The tangent space of a finite group therefore has dimension zero. Hence a Lie algebra cannot distinguish finite groups.

Example 1.4 (General linear groups). The group of invertible real matrices is GL⁡n(R)={g∈Mn×n(R):det⁡g≠0}.\GL_n(\R)=\{g\in M_{n\times n}(\R):\det g\ne0\}.GLn​(R)={g∈Mn×n​(R):detg=0}. The entries of a matrix product are polynomials in the entries of the factors, so matrix multiplication is smooth. Inversion is smooth because g−1=adj⁡(g)det⁡g.g^{-1}=\frac{\operatorname{adj}(g)}{\det g}.g−1=detgadj(g)​. The same argument applies to GL⁡n(C)\GL_n(\C)GLn​(C). A complex Lie group has complex charts and holomorphic group operations. The group GL⁡n(C)\GL_n(\C)GLn​(C) has this extra structure. Its complex dimension is n2n^2n2. A group of complex matrices need not be a complex Lie group. Its defining conditions may involve complex conjugation.

We verify the manifold structure of SU⁡(2)\SU(2)SU(2) directly; Exercise Sheet 1 treats U⁡(n)\UU(n)U(n).

A manifold is connected if it cannot be written as the union of two nonempty disjoint open subsets. For example, the orthogonal group is O⁡(n)={g∈Mn×n(R):gTg=I},n≥1.\operatorname{O}(n)=\{g\in M_{n\times n}(\R):g^{\mathsf T}g=I\}, \qquad n\geq1.O(n)={g∈Mn×n​(R):gTg=I},n≥1. 1.4 Translations and the bracket

A tangent space at one point is only a vector space, and it has no product. Vector fields do have a commutator, because they act on functions by differentiation. Left translation carries a tangent vector at eee to a vector field on GGG. We therefore obtain a bracket on the tangent space. Lecture 2 shows that this bracket measures how far small group elements fail to commute.

Summary and reading

A Lie group has smooth multiplication and inversion. Its tangent space at the identity is a real Lie algebra. Left translation determines the bracket. For embedded matrix groups, the bracket is the matrix commutator. The algebra records local information. Distinct groups can have the same tangent algebra.

Read Hall, second edition, Sections 1.1–1.5 and 3.3–3.6, and Bröcker–tom Dieck, Chapter I, Sections 1–2. The regular level-set theorem and the embedded-submanifold properties are the geometric imports, cited from Warner, Chapter 1. Lecture 2 develops the exponential map and connects these tangent vectors to one-parameter subgroups.
