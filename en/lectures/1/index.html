The Lie algebras in the earlier course were complex. The tangent algebra of a real Lie group is a real Lie algebra. We will always state the field of scalars.

1.1 Smooth coordinates

A sphere cannot be described by one set of Euclidean coordinates without omitting a point or introducing a singularity. It can be described by several overlapping coordinate systems. A smooth manifold formalises this local description.

A topology specifies which subsets are open. For subsets of Euclidean space we use the usual relative topology. A neighbourhood of a point is a set containing an open set around that point. A topological space is Hausdorff if distinct points have disjoint open neighbourhoods. It is second countable if its topology has a countable basis, namely a countable collection of open sets such that every open set is a union of some of them.

A map between topological spaces is continuous if the inverse image of every open set is open. A homeomorphism is a continuous bijection whose inverse is continuous.

Definition 1.1 (Smooth manifold). A smooth real manifold of dimension ddd is a topological space MMM covered by coordinate charts φ ⁣:U⟶V⊆Rd.\varphi\colon U\longrightarrow V\subseteq\R^d.φ:U⟶V⊆Rd. Each chart has open domain U⊆MU\subseteq MU⊆M and is a homeomorphism onto an open set VVV. Where the domains of two charts φ\varphiφ and ψ\psiψ overlap, the coordinate change ψ∘φ−1\psi\circ\varphi^{-1}ψ∘φ−1 is infinitely differentiable. We require MMM to be Hausdorff and second countable.

The Hausdorff and second-countability conditions exclude topological pathologies. All the matrix groups in this lecture satisfy them as subspaces of a Euclidean space. A smooth map between manifolds is a continuous map whose coordinate expressions are infinitely differentiable on their open domains.

Example 1.2 (A coordinate on the circle). The unit circle is S1={(x,y)∈R2:x2+y2=1}.S^1=\{(x,y)\in\R^2:x^2+y^2=1\}.S1={(x,y)∈R2:x2+y2=1}. On the open right semicircle, use yyy as the coordinate. Its inverse chart is y⟼(1−y2,y),−1<y<1.y\longmapsto\bigl(\sqrt{1-y^2},y\bigr),\qquad -1<y<1.y⟼(1−y2​,y),−1<y<1. The other three semicircles have similar charts. The coordinate changes are smooth on their overlaps. Thus the circle is a one-dimensional real manifold, although it lies in a two-dimensional ambient space.

The coordinate yyy can also describe a velocity. For a real number aaa, the curve on the circle γa(t)=(1−a2t2,at)\gamma_a(t)=\bigl(\sqrt{1-a^2t^2},at\bigr)γa​(t)=(1−a2t2​,at) is defined near zero. It starts at (1,0)(1,0)(1,0) and has ambient derivative (0,a)(0,a)(0,a). The derivative of its yyy-coordinate is aaa. Thus a one-dimensional tangent space can appear as a line inside a larger ambient space.

An embedded submanifold has the relative topology and is locally a coordinate subspace in ambient smooth coordinates. We import its standard properties. Its inclusion is smooth with injective differential. Products of embedded submanifolds are embedded in the corresponding product. Thus smooth ambient maps restrict smoothly to these submanifolds. A smooth ambient map whose image lies in an embedded submanifold is smooth as a map into that submanifold. The differential of the inclusion identifies its tangent vectors with ambient velocities.

We also use one result from multivariable calculus. Let U⊆RNU\subseteq\R^NU⊆RN be open, and suppose a smooth map F ⁣:U→RrF\colon U\to\R^rF:U→Rr has surjective differential at every point of F−1(c)F^{-1}(c)F−1(c). The regular level-set theorem says that F−1(c)F^{-1}(c)F−1(c) is an embedded submanifold of UUU of dimension N−rN-rN−r, with the relative topology. The tangent space of F−1(c)F^{-1}(c)F−1(c) at a point ppp is ker⁡dFp\ker dF_pkerdFp​. The regular level-set theorem and the embedded-submanifold properties are cited from Warner, Chapter 1, sections “Submanifolds, Diffeomorphisms, and the Inverse Function Theorem” and “Implicit Function Theorems” (Theorem 1.38).

1.2 Smooth groups

Product charts φ×ψ\varphi\times\psiφ×ψ on U×VU\times VU×V give M×NM\times NM×N a smooth manifold structure of dimension dim⁡M+dim⁡N\dim M+\dim NdimM+dimN. A finite product of Hausdorff, second-countable spaces has both properties.

Definition 1.3 (Lie group). A real Lie group is a group GGG that is also a smooth real manifold, with smooth multiplication and inversion: G×G⟶G,(g,h)⟼gh,G⟶G,g⟼g−1.G\times G\longrightarrow G,\quad (g,h)\longmapsto gh, \qquad G\longrightarrow G,\quad g\longmapsto g^{-1}.G×G⟶G,(g,h)⟼gh,G⟶G,g⟼g−1. A Lie group homomorphism is a smooth group homomorphism.

The additive group Rd\R^dRd is a Lie group. Any finite group with the discrete topology is also a Lie group, since each one-point subset is the domain of a chart modelled on R0\R^0R0. The tangent space of a finite group therefore has dimension zero. Hence a Lie algebra cannot distinguish finite groups.

Example 1.4 (General linear groups). The group of invertible real matrices is GL⁡n(R)={g∈Mn×n(R):det⁡g≠0}.\GL_n(\R)=\{g\in M_{n\times n}(\R):\det g\ne0\}.GLn​(R)={g∈Mn×n​(R):detg=0}. The determinant is continuous, so GL⁡n(R)\GL_n(\R)GLn​(R) is open in Rn2\R^{n^2}Rn2. Hence its dimension is n2n^2n2. The entries of a matrix product are polynomials in the entries of the factors, so matrix multiplication is smooth. Inversion is smooth because g−1=adj⁡(g)det⁡g.g^{-1}=\frac{\operatorname{adj}(g)}{\det g}.g−1=detgadj(g)​. The same argument applies to GL⁡n(C)\GL_n(\C)GLn​(C). The real and imaginary parts of the entries give its coordinates, so its dimension as a real manifold is 2n22n^22n2.

A complex Lie group has complex charts and holomorphic group operations. The group GL⁡n(C)\GL_n(\C)GLn​(C) has this extra structure. Its complex dimension is n2n^2n2. A group of complex matrices need not be a complex Lie group. Its defining conditions may involve complex conjugation.

For example, the unitary and special unitary groups are U⁡(n)={U∈Mn×n(C):U∗U=I},SU⁡(n)={U∈U⁡(n):det⁡U=1}.\UU(n)=\{U\in M_{n\times n}(\C):U^*U=I\}, \qquad \SU(n)=\{U\in\UU(n):\det U=1\}.U(n)={U∈Mn×n​(C):U∗U=I},SU(n)={U∈U(n):detU=1}. We verify the manifold structure of SU⁡(2)\SU(2)SU(2) directly; Exercise Sheet 1 treats U⁡(n)\UU(n)U(n).

A manifold is connected if it cannot be written as the union of two nonempty disjoint open subsets. For example, the orthogonal group is O⁡(n)={g∈Mn×n(R):gTg=I},n≥1.\operatorname{O}(n)=\{g\in M_{n\times n}(\R):g^{\mathsf T}g=I\}, \qquad n\geq1.O(n)={g∈Mn×n​(R):gTg=I},n≥1. Summary and reading

A Lie group has smooth multiplication and inversion. Its tangent space at the identity is a real Lie algebra. Left translation determines the bracket. For embedded matrix groups, the bracket is the matrix commutator. The algebra records local information. Distinct groups can have the same tangent algebra.

Read Hall, second edition, Sections 1.1–1.5 and 3.3–3.6, and Bröcker–tom Dieck, Chapter I, Sections 1–2. The regular level-set theorem and the embedded-submanifold properties are the geometric imports, cited from Warner, Chapter 1. Lecture 2 develops the exponential map and connects these tangent vectors to one-parameter subgroups.
