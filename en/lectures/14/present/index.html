Lecture 14

Representative functions and tensor reconstruction

This lecture asks what information about a compact Lie group KKK is contained in all its representations. We collect all matrix coefficients into one algebra R(K)\Rcoeff(K)R(K). The group law becomes three maps on this algebra. We then state how KKK is recovered, and what is recovered when the unitary structure is forgotten.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Prove that R(K)\Rcoeff(K)R(K) is a ∗*∗-algebra whose products come from tensor products and whose conjugation comes from conjugate representations.
  • Define Δ\DeltaΔ, ε\varepsilonε, SSS and prove the Hopf algebra identities, also in matrix form.
  • State Tannaka–Krein reconstruction with the unitary fibre functor, and contrast it with the complex reconstruction of KCK_\CKC​.
  • Compute these structures for U⁡(1)\UU(1)U(1) and SU⁡(2)\SU(2)SU(2), and explain why Rep⁡fd(K)\Rep_{\mathrm{fd}}(K)Repfd​(K) is not a fusion category when KKK is infinite.

Throughout, KKK is a compact Lie group. The category Rep⁡fd(K)\Rep_{\mathrm{fd}}(K)Repfd​(K) has as objects the finite-dimensional continuous complex representations (ΠV,V)(\Pi_V,V)(ΠV​,V). Its morphisms are the KKK-equivariant linear maps.

In other words, Δ\DeltaΔ, ε\varepsilonε and SSS give the multiplication, the identity and the inverses in X(K)\mathcal X(K)X(K). To recover KKK we must know that every ∗*∗-character is an evaluation. We now describe the same question in categorical terms.

The data of the unitary fibre functor are the following.

  1. The category Rep⁡fd(K)\Rep_{\mathrm{fd}}(K)Repfd​(K) with its tensor product and unit object C\CC.

  2. The functor ω\omegaω, which remembers the underlying vector spaces and linear maps.

  3. On each ω(V)\omega(V)ω(V), the set of KKK-invariant inner products. On the algebra side these inner products correspond to the ∗*∗-structure.

Each g∈Kg\in Kg∈K gives the family with θV=ΠV(g)\theta_V=\Pi_V(g)θV​=ΠV​(g). This family is natural because morphisms are equivariant. It is monoidal by the definition of V⊗WV\otimes WV⊗W. It is unitary because the inner products are KKK-invariant.

The SU⁡(2)\SU(2)SU(2) model, Example 14.12, finds ggg directly. In general we cite this step.

14.5 The representation category

We use the notions of a rigid and of a symmetric monoidal category from the prerequisite course. Rigidity requires duals, with evaluation and coevaluation maps that satisfy the zigzag identities. A symmetry is a natural isomorphism τV,W ⁣:V⊗W→W⊗V\tau_{V,W}\colon V\otimes W\to W\otimes VτV,W​:V⊗W→W⊗V with τW,VτV,W=id⁡\tau_{W,V}\tau_{V,W}=\idτW,V​τV,W​=id, compatible with associativity.

Definition 14.13. A fusion category is a C\CC-linear semisimple rigid monoidal category with finite-dimensional morphism spaces, simple unit object, and finitely many isomorphism classes of simple objects.

The category Rep⁡fd(K)\Rep_{\mathrm{fd}}(K)Repfd​(K) is semisimple (Lecture 5) with simple unit C\CC and finite-dimensional morphism spaces. It is rigid, with the dual representation and evV(λ⊗v)=λ(v),coevV(1)=∑iei⊗ei.\mathrm{ev}_V(\lambda\otimes v)=\lambda(v),\qquad \mathrm{coev}_V(1)=\sum_ie_i\otimes e^i.evV​(λ⊗v)=λ(v),coevV​(1)=i∑​ei​⊗ei. It is symmetric with τ(v⊗w)=w⊗v\tau(v\otimes w)=w\otimes vτ(v⊗w)=w⊗v. Exercise 14.6 checks these claims.

Proposition 14.14. If KKK is infinite, then Irr⁡(K)\Irr(K)Irr(K) is infinite and Rep⁡fd(K)\Rep_{\mathrm{fd}}(K)Repfd​(K) is not a fusion category. In particular, both conclusions hold when KKK is connected and nontrivial.

Finally, a finite Hausdorff space is discrete. Hence a connected Hausdorff space with at least two points is infinite, and the first part applies to connected nontrivial KKK. 

For a finite group the representation category is a fusion category (Exercise 14.2).

Summary

Matrix coefficients of all finite-dimensional continuous representations of the compact Lie group KKK form the commutative ∗*∗-algebra R(K)\Rcoeff(K)R(K). Tensor products give its products and conjugate representations give its involution. The maps Δf(g,h)=f(gh)\Delta f(g,h)=f(gh)Δf(g,h)=f(gh), εf=f(e)\varepsilon f=f(e)εf=f(e) and Sf(g)=f(g−1)Sf(g)=f(g^{-1})Sf(g)=f(g−1) make it a Hopf ∗*∗-algebra. On coefficients they are D(gh)=D(g)D(h)D(gh)=D(g)D(h)D(gh)=D(g)D(h), D(e)=1D(e)=1D(e)=1 and D(g−1)=D(g)−1D(g^{-1})=D(g)^{-1}D(g−1)=D(g)−1. The ∗*∗-characters, or unitary tensor automorphisms of ω\omegaω, recover KKK. All characters, or all tensor automorphisms, give KCK_\CKC​. The category Rep⁡fd(K)\Rep_{\mathrm{fd}}(K)Repfd​(K) is rigid and symmetric. When KKK is infinite, it has infinitely many simple objects and is not a fusion category.
