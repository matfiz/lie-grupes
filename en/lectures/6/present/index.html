Lecture 6

Compact Structure and Maximal Tori

This lecture explains why conjugacy in a compact connected group can be studied on a torus. First, an averaged inner product splits the Lie algebra. Next, maximal tori meet every conjugacy class. Finally, a finite Weyl group records which elements of the torus are conjugate.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Average an inner product and prove k=z(k)⊕[k,k]\algk=\algz(\algk)\oplus[\algk,\algk]k=z(k)⊕[k,k] with [k,k][\algk,\algk][k,k] semisimple.
  • Prove that maximal tori are conjugate, explain the strategy of the torus theorem, and deduce that exp⁡\expexp is surjective for compact connected groups.
  • Compute NK(T)/TN_K(T)/TNK​(T)/T for U⁡(n)\UU(n)U(n) and SU⁡(n)\SU(n)SU(n) and match it with the algebraic Weyl group.
  • Locate where these statements fail for SL⁡(2,R)\SL(2,\R)SL(2,R).

Throughout, KKK is a compact Lie group with Lie algebra k\algkk. Its Haar probability measure dgdgdg is left and right invariant (Lecture 5). Connectedness of KKK is assumed only where a statement says so.

  1. Every g∈Kg\in Kg∈K lies in xTx−1xTx^{-1}xTx−1 for some x∈Kx\in Kx∈K.

6.3 An outline of the torus theorem via mapping degree

Surjectivity of exp⁡\expexp needs a global argument. We outline the proof in Hall (H §§11.3–11.5).

We count how many times Φ\PhiΦ covers a point, with signs. A point yyy is a regular value of a smooth map if the differential of the map is invertible at every point of the inverse image of yyy. For a smooth map between compact connected oriented manifolds of equal dimension, the signed count of preimages is the same at every regular value. A point outside the image would have count zero. So it is enough to show that the count is nonzero at one regular value. On a circle, an orientation is a direction of travel. The general definition is part of the cited theory.

Together with the cited orbit theorem for W(K,T)W(K,T)W(K,T), it identifies the conjugacy classes of KKK with T/W(K,T)T/W(K,T)T/W(K,T). The Weyl group is finite. For U⁡(n)\UU(n)U(n) and SU⁡(n)\SU(n)SU(n) it is isomorphic to SnS_nSn​. Compactness is essential, as the connected group SL⁡(2,R)\SL(2,\R)SL(2,R) shows.
