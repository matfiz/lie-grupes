Lecture 4

Covering groups, integration and descent

We ask when a finite-dimensional Lie algebra representation is the differential of a representation of a given connected Lie group. On a simply connected group it always is. On other connected groups we integrate on the universal covering group and then test a central kernel. For example, spin one half is a representation of SU⁡(2)\SU(2)SU(2) and not of SO⁡(3)\SO(3)SO(3).

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Describe the universal covering group by path classes and use the lifting theorem.
  • Prove that a discrete normal subgroup of a connected group is central.
  • State Lie’s integration theorem and Lie III with their hypotheses, and explain path transport.
  • Prove the descent criterion and apply it to R→U⁡(1)\R\to\UU(1)R→U(1) and SU⁡(2)→SO⁡(3)\SU(2)\to\SO(3)SU(2)→SO(3).

Lie groups are finite-dimensional, real, Hausdorff and second countable. From Lecture 1–Lecture 3 we use three facts. The map exp⁡\expexp is a diffeomorphism near 000. A connected Lie group is generated by any neighbourhood of eee. A continuous homomorphism Φ\PhiΦ of Lie groups is smooth and satisfies Φ(exp⁡X)=exp⁡dΦ(X)\Phi(\exp X)=\exp d\Phi(X)Φ(expX)=expdΦ(X). Hence two continuous homomorphisms on a connected Lie group with equal differentials are equal.

4.1 Loops, homotopy and covering groups

Definition 4.1. A path in a space XXX is a continuous map γ ⁣:[0,1]→X\gamma\colon[0,1]\to Xγ:[0,1]→X. It is a loop at xxx if γ(0)=γ(1)=x\gamma(0)=\gamma(1)=xγ(0)=γ(1)=x. A space is path connected if any two of its points are joined by a path. It is locally path connected if every neighbourhood of a point contains a path-connected neighbourhood of that point. Every manifold is locally path connected, and [0,1][0,1][0,1] and [0,1]2[0,1]^2[0,1]2 are both path connected and locally path connected. A topological group is a group with a topology for which multiplication and inversion are continuous.

Proposition 4.6. Let GGG be a connected topological group. Let NNN be a normal subgroup of GGG that is discrete in the subspace topology. Then NNN lies in the centre of GGG.

4.2 Lie’s integration theorem and Lie III

Theorem 4.7 (Lie’s integration theorem; cited, with a sketch). Let GGG and HHH be Lie groups with Lie algebras g\gg and h\mathfrak hh. Assume that GGG is connected and simply connected. Let φ ⁣:g→h\varphi\colon\g\to\mathfrak hφ:g→h be a homomorphism of real Lie algebras. Then there is a unique smooth homomorphism Φ ⁣:G→H\Phi\colon G\to HΦ:G→H with dΦ=φd\Phi=\varphidΦ=φ. It satisfies Φ(exp⁡X)=exp⁡φ(X)\Phi(\exp X)=\exp\varphi(X)Φ(expX)=expφ(X).

We cite this from H §5.7 for matrix groups. For general Lie groups we cite Knapp Beyond, Sections I.10–11, Elementary theory of Lie groups and Covering groups. For representations take H=GL⁡(V)H=\GL(V)H=GL(V) with dim⁡CV<∞\dim_\C V<\inftydimC​V<∞.

Theorem 4.8 (Lie III; cited). Every finite-dimensional real Lie algebra is the Lie algebra of a connected, simply connected Lie group. This group is unique up to isomorphism.

We cite existence from H §5.10. There Ado’s theorem and H §5.9 give a connected Lie subgroup of a general linear group with the given Lie algebra. This subgroup need not be closed. The universal covering group of this subgroup is the required group. Uniqueness follows by integrating an isomorphism and its inverse with Lie’s integration theorem, Theorem 4.7. The universal covering group of SL⁡(2,R)\SL(2,\R)SL(2,R) is not isomorphic to any matrix Lie group (H §4.8). Hence Lie III needs abstract Lie groups.

4.3 Descent through a covering kernel

Summary

A connected Lie group GGG has a universal covering group p ⁣:G~→Gp\colon\tilde G\to Gp:G~→G. Its kernel Γ≅π1(G,e)\Gamma\cong\pi_1(G,e)Γ≅π1​(G,e) is discrete and central. Let φ\varphiφ be a representation of the Lie algebra of GGG on a finite-dimensional complex vector space. Then φ∘dp\varphi\circ dpφ∘dp integrates to a representation Π~\tilde\PiΠ~ of G~\tilde GG~. The representation φ\varphiφ is the differential of a representation of GGG exactly when Γ\GammaΓ acts trivially through Π~\tilde\PiΠ~. For R→U⁡(1)\R\to\UU(1)R→U(1) this is the period condition λ∈iZ\lambda\in i\mathbb{Z}λ∈iZ. For SU⁡(2)→SO⁡(3)\SU(2)\to\SO(3)SU(2)→SO(3) the condition holds exactly for even mmm, that is, for integer spin.
