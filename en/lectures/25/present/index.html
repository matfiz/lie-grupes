Lecture 25

Poincaré representations and little groups

This lecture reduces the classification of positive-energy irreducible unitary representations of the Poincaré group to little groups. It makes the massive and finite-helicity sectors explicit. Translations are diagonalised by momenta. Lorentz transformations move momenta along orbits. The orbit and an irreducible unitary representation of the stabiliser of one of its points determine the representation up to unitary equivalence.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Construct the group R4⋊SL⁡(2,C)\R^4\rtimes\SL(2,\C)R4⋊SL(2,C) and its action on momenta.
  • Classify the momentum orbits and compute the massive and massless little groups.
  • State Mackey’s classification with its hypotheses, and separate the finite-helicity, continuous-spin and zero-momentum sectors.
  • Verify the massive spin model and name the steps it omits.

Summary

The Poincaré cover is G=R4⋊SL⁡(2,C)G=\R^4\rtimes\SL(2,\C)G=R4⋊SL(2,C). Translations act through momentum characters e−ip⋅ae^{-ip\cdot a}e−ip⋅a, and SL⁡(2,C)\SL(2,\C)SL(2,C) moves momenta by Λ\LambdaΛ. The orbits are mass shells, light cones, spacelike hyperboloids and {0}\{0\}{0}. Mackey’s theorem reduces the classification to little groups. Up to unitary equivalence, irreducible unitary representations with EEE concentrated on Xm+X_m^+Xm+​ are labelled by an SU⁡(2)\SU(2)SU(2) spin n/2n/2n/2 with n≥0n\ge0n≥0. Finite-helicity massless representations have trivial little-group translations. Continuous-spin sectors also have positive energy and are separate. The zero-momentum orbit {0}\{0\}{0} gives a further separate sector.
