Poincaré representations and little groups

What this lecture does

This lecture reduces the classification of positive-energy irreducible unitary representations of the Poincaré group to little groups. It makes the massive and finite-helicity sectors explicit. Translations are diagonalised by momenta. Lorentz transformations move momenta along orbits. The orbit and an irreducible unitary representation of the stabiliser of one of its points determine the representation up to unitary equivalence.

Summary

The Poincaré cover is G=R4⋊SL⁡(2,C)G=\R^4\rtimes\SL(2,\C)G=R4⋊SL(2,C). Translations act through momentum characters e−ip⋅ae^{-ip\cdot a}e−ip⋅a, and SL⁡(2,C)\SL(2,\C)SL(2,C) moves momenta by Λ\LambdaΛ. The orbits are mass shells, light cones, spacelike hyperboloids and {0}\{0\}{0}. The projection E(S)E(S)E(S) selects the part of the space with momentum in SSS, and in the massive model it is multiplication by an indicator function. Mackey’s theorem reduces the classification to little groups. It already gives the irreducible unitary representations of the ax+bax+bax+b group and of the Heisenberg group. Up to unitary equivalence, irreducible unitary representations with EEE concentrated on Xm+X_m^+Xm+​ are labelled by an SU⁡(2)\SU(2)SU(2) spin n/2n/2n/2 with n≥0n\ge0n≥0. Finite-helicity massless representations have trivial little-group translations. Continuous-spin sectors also have positive energy and are separate. The zero-momentum orbit {0}\{0\}{0} gives a further separate sector.
