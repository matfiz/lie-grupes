Subgroups, quotients and homogeneous spaces

What this lecture does

A subgroup of a Lie group need not be a submanifold, and a coset space need not be a manifold. Closedness of the subgroup makes the coset space a manifold. Normality is a separate condition, needed for a group law. We test both conditions on an irrational line in a torus and on two descriptions of the sphere S2S^2S2.

3.1 Closed and immersed subgroups

A subset SSS of a manifold MMM carries the subspace topology of Lecture 1: its open sets are the sets U∩SU\cap SU∩S with UUU open in MMM. The subset SSS is closed if M∖SM\setminus SM∖S is open. In a manifold, SSS is closed exactly when SSS contains the limit of each convergent sequence of its points.

The closure S‾\overline SS consists of the points whose every open neighbourhood meets SSS. It is the smallest closed set containing SSS. The set SSS is dense if S‾=M\overline S=MS=M. By the definition of closure, SSS is dense exactly when SSS meets every nonempty open subset of MMM.

In a manifold, every point of S‾\overline SS is a limit of a sequence in SSS. To obtain the sequence, choose points of SSS in chart balls of radii tending to zero.

We also need compactness, in the form of finite subcovers. Lecture 5 uses this form to prove strict positivity of the Haar integral.

Definition 3.1 (Compact space). A topological space is compact if every cover by open sets has a finite subcover.

A continuous image of a compact space is compact. Indeed, the inverse images of an open cover of the image form an open cover of the space. A finite subfamily of them covers the space, so the corresponding open sets cover the image.

A subset of Rn\R^nRn is compact exactly when it is closed and bounded (Heine–Borel). For subsets of Rn\R^nRn this agrees with the definition by sequences in Linear Algebra for Physics. For a set of matrices, closed and bounded must be read in the matrix space. For example, the set {tI:0<t≤1}\{tI:0<t\le1\}{tI:0<t≤1} is bounded and it is closed in GL⁡n(R)\GL_n(\R)GLn​(R). It is not compact, because its closure in the matrix space contains the zero matrix.

Definition 3.2 (Immersions and embeddings). A smooth map f ⁣:N→Mf\colon N\to Mf:N→M is an immersion if dfpdf_pdfp​ is injective for every p∈Np\in Np∈N. It is an embedding if it is an injective immersion and a homeomorphism from NNN onto f(N)f(N)f(N) with the subspace topology.

The regular level sets of Lecture 1 are images of embeddings. An injective immersion can fail to be an embedding. The reason is that the topology carried over from NNN to f(N)f(N)f(N) can be finer than the subspace topology, that is, it can have more open sets.

Definition 3.3 (Immersed and embedded subgroups). Let GGG be a Lie group. An immersed subgroup of GGG is a subgroup HHH with a Lie group structure for which the inclusion ι ⁣:H→G\iota\colon H\to Gι:H→G is an immersion. It is an embedded subgroup if ι\iotaι is an embedding.

The differential dιed\iota_edιe​ identifies Lie⁡(H)\Lie(H)Lie(H) with a subspace h⊆g\mathfrak h\subseteq\gh⊆g. By Lecture 2, the differential of a Lie group homomorphism preserves brackets. Hence h\mathfrak hh is a real Lie subalgebra of g\gg.

Theorem 3.4 (Closed-subgroup theorem; cited). Let GGG be a Lie group and let H⊆GH\subseteq GH⊆G be a subgroup that is a closed subset. Then HHH is an embedded submanifold of GGG, and with this structure HHH is a Lie group. Its Lie algebra is h={X∈g:exp⁡(tX)∈H for all t∈R}.\mathfrak h=\{X\in\g:\exp(tX)\in H\text{ for all }t\in\R\}.h={X∈g:exp(tX)∈H for all t∈R}.

The hypothesis concerns only the subset HHH. Smoothness of HHH is a conclusion. For example, U⁡(n)\UU(n)U(n) and SU⁡(n)\SU(n)SU(n) are closed in GL⁡n(C)\GL_n(\C)GLn​(C), because they are defined by continuous equations.

A related regularity theorem removes a hypothesis from homomorphisms.

Theorem 3.5 (Automatic smoothness; cited). Every continuous homomorphism between finite-dimensional real Lie groups is smooth.

See Bröcker–tom Dieck, Chapter I, Section 3, Proposition (3.12). In particular, finite-dimensional continuous representations have differentials. We use this fact from Lecture 4 onwards. In particular, by the lemma on smooth one-parameter subgroups in Lecture 2, every continuous one-parameter subgroup of a Lie group has the form t↦exp⁡(tX)t\mapsto\exp(tX)t↦exp(tX).

Theorem 3.6 (Subgroups and subalgebras; cited). Let GGG be a Lie group and let h⊆g\mathfrak h\subseteq\gh⊆g be a real Lie subalgebra. There is exactly one connected immersed subgroup H⊆GH\subseteq GH⊆G with Lie⁡(H)=h\Lie(H)=\mathfrak hLie(H)=h. As a set, HHH is the subgroup generated by exp⁡(h)\exp(\mathfrak h)exp(h). Every connected immersed subgroup arises in this way from its own Lie algebra.

Warner, Chapter 3, proves Theorems 3.4 and 3.6 for general Lie groups. Hall, Section 5.9, proves Theorem 3.6 for matrix Lie groups. Kirillov Jr., Chapters 2 and 3, states these two subgroup theorems.

The two theorems run in opposite directions. The first starts with a subset and concludes that it is a manifold. The second starts with a subalgebra and constructs a group. That group need not be closed or embedded, so the word “immersed” in Theorem 3.6 cannot be replaced by “embedded”. The next section gives an example of a connected immersed subgroup that is neither closed nor embedded.

3.3 Coset spaces, actions and quotient groups

Summary and reading

A closed subgroup of a Lie group is an embedded subgroup. A real Lie subalgebra determines a connected immersed subgroup, which need not be closed or embedded. For example, the irrational line is dense and not embedded. For closed HHH, the coset space G/HG/HG/H is a manifold whose tangent space at eHeHeH is isomorphic to g/h\g/\mathfrak hg/h. If a Lie group GGG acts smoothly and transitively on a manifold MMM, then MMM is equivariantly diffeomorphic to G/GpG/G_pG/Gp​ for each p∈Mp\in Mp∈M. Normality is a further condition, needed for G/HG/HG/H to be a group. The sphere is diffeomorphic to both SO⁡(3)/SO⁡(2)\SO(3)/\SO(2)SO(3)/SO(2) and SU⁡(2)/U⁡(1)\SU(2)/\UU(1)SU(2)/U(1). The two models have different isotropy actions.
