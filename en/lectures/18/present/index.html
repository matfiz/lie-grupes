Lecture 18

Heisenberg group and the Weyl relations

The Heisenberg group is the simplest noncommutative nilpotent Lie group. This lecture builds its Schrödinger representation on L2(R)L^2(\R)L2(R) from translations and modulations, and proves the Weyl relations, which describe how translations and modulations commute up to a phase. On this representation, the bracket [X,Y]=Z[X,Y]=Z[X,Y]=Z becomes the commutation relation [Q,P]=iℏ[Q,P]=i\hbar[Q,P]=iℏ of quantum mechanics. We then treat the Stone–von Neumann theorem. It says that a fixed nonzero central character, which records the scalars by which the centre acts, determines one irreducible unitary representation up to unitary equivalence. We prove the irreducibility of the Schrödinger representation from an explicit rank-one operator, and we cite the uniqueness. For central character zero, the irreducibles are one-dimensional characters, and the translation representation on L2(R)L^2(\R)L2(R) contains none of them.

Summary

The Heisenberg group has centre Z(H)≅RZ(H)\cong\RZ(H)≅R, and every commutator lies in it. Translations and modulations on L2(R)L^2(\R)L2(R) form the Schrödinger representation Πλ\Pi_\lambdaΠλ​, which satisfies the Weyl relations. For λ≠0\lambda\ne0λ=0 the Gaussian form computes an explicit rank-one projection, and this proves that Πλ\Pi_\lambdaΠλ​ is irreducible. The uniqueness statement of the Stone–von Neumann theorem, cited, says that for λ≠0\lambda\neq0λ=0 every strongly continuous unitary representation on a nonzero separable space with central character λ\lambdaλ is a Hilbert direct sum of copies of Πλ\Pi_\lambdaΠλ​, up to unitary equivalence. At λ=0\lambda=0λ=0 the irreducibles are the characters χα,β\chi_{\alpha,\beta}χα,β​, and the translation representation Π0\Pi_0Π0​ contains none of them.
