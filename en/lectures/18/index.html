Lecture 18

Heisenberg group and the Weyl relations

What this lecture does

The Heisenberg group is the simplest noncommutative nilpotent Lie group. This lecture builds its Schrödinger representation on L2(R)L^2(\R)L2(R) from translations and modulations, and proves the Weyl relations, which describe how translations and modulations commute up to a phase. We then state the Stone–von Neumann theorem. It says that a fixed nonzero central character, which records the scalars by which the centre acts, determines one irreducible unitary representation up to unitary equivalence. For central character zero, the irreducibles are one-dimensional characters, and the translation representation on L2(R)L^2(\R)L2(R) contains none of them.

Theorem 18.9 (Stone–von Neumann). Fix λ≠0\lambda\neq0λ=0.

  1. The Schrödinger representation Πλ\Pi_\lambdaΠλ​ is irreducible.

  2. Let ρ\rhoρ be a strongly continuous unitary representation of HHH on a nonzero separable Hilbert space H\mathcal HH with central character λ\lambdaλ. Then H\mathcal HH is a Hilbert direct sum of mutually orthogonal closed ρ\rhoρ-invariant subspaces. On each summand ρ\rhoρ is unitarily equivalent to Πλ\Pi_\lambdaΠλ​.

We cite the Stone–von Neumann theorem, Theorem 18.9, from Kirillov, Lectures on the Orbit Method, Chapter 2.

The Hilbert space of an irreducible representation of HHH is automatically separable. By irreducibility, every nonzero vector is cyclic. By strong continuity, the closed span of the orbit of such a vector is already generated by its orbit under a countable dense subset of HHH. This closed span is the whole space, and it is separable.

The Stone–von Neumann theorem, Theorem 18.9, has the following consequences.

  • Every irreducible unitary representation of HHH with central character λ≠0\lambda\neq0λ=0 is unitarily equivalent to Πλ\Pi_\lambdaΠλ​.

  • By the lemma on Weyl pairs, Lemma 18.8, every Weyl pair with parameter λ≠0\lambda\neq0λ=0 on a nonzero separable space is equivalent to a direct sum of copies of (U,Vλ)(U,V_\lambda)(U,Vλ​). In particular, every Weyl pair with parameter 1/ℏ1/\hbar1/ℏ on a nonzero separable space is unitarily equivalent to a direct sum of copies of (exp⁡(iaP/ℏ),exp⁡(ibQ/ℏ))(\exp(iaP/\hbar),\exp(ibQ/\hbar))(exp(iaP/ℏ),exp(ibQ/ℏ)).

  • Distinct values of λ\lambdaλ give inequivalent representations Πλ\Pi_\lambdaΠλ​ (Exercise 18.6(c) on inequivalence).

Summary

The Heisenberg group has centre Z(H)≅RZ(H)\cong\RZ(H)≅R, and every commutator lies in it. Translations and modulations on L2(R)L^2(\R)L2(R) form the Schrödinger representation Πλ\Pi_\lambdaΠλ​, which satisfies the Weyl relations. The Stone–von Neumann theorem, cited, says that for λ≠0\lambda\neq0λ=0 every strongly continuous unitary representation on a nonzero separable space with central character λ\lambdaλ is a Hilbert direct sum of copies of Πλ\Pi_\lambdaΠλ​, up to unitary equivalence. At λ=0\lambda=0λ=0 the irreducibles are the characters χα,β\chi_{\alpha,\beta}χα,β​, and the translation representation Π0\Pi_0Π0​ contains none of them.
