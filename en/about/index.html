About this site

The companion site to Lie Groups and Their Representation Theory.

What is here

28 lectures released so far, with 85 definitions, 196 numbered results, 162 proofs, 34 worked examples and 2 figures. The pages are generated from the same LaTeX source as the PDF notes.

This course has two parts of fourteen lectures. Part I studies the global structure of Lie groups and the representations of compact groups. Part II studies selected noncompact groups and the geometry behind their representations. It includes the Heisenberg, Lorentz and Poincaré groups. The final lecture prepares for a later course on quantum groups.

The prerequisites are Linear Algebra for Physics, Finite Groups and Their Representations, Lie Algebras and Their Representations, and basic category language. Ordinary multivariable calculus and elementary ordinary differential equations are also assumed. Each lecture is intended for about ninety minutes of study; this is a planning target. Each lecture has self-checks with their answers. Every lecture has an exercise sheet.

English is the primary language. All 28 lectures are also available in Lithuanian. The exercise sheets are in English only.

How much is proved

Not every result is proved in the notes. The course plan uses four labels. [P] means that the result is proved in the lecture. [S] means that the argument is sketched and the omitted step is identified. [C] means that a theorem is cited. [I] means that a result comes from a prerequisite course. A cited theorem is an input to the argument that follows it.

The notes introduce the geometry and analysis needed at each stage. They do not replace courses on differential geometry, topology or functional analysis. Long existence theorems are stated with their hypotheses and a source. The main calculations are carried out here.

Questions and solutions

An exercise is a question, and a solution is the full worked method. The exercise sheets on this site, and their PDFs, are the questions-only edition, in English. Worked solutions are not published on this site. Some sheets include an optional extension. It is marked explicitly and is not needed for the next lecture.

The lecture self-checks are different. Their answers are part of the lecture and stay visible, on the page and in the PDF.

Found a mistake?

Please report it to the course's author. Corrections during a course's first run are genuinely valuable: each is fixed at source and republished.

Colophon

Built as a static site from LaTeX. Pandoc preserves the notes' own definition, theorem, example and remark structure. KaTeX renders the mathematics at build time with the course's own notation macros, read from the style file the PDFs load. The diagrams are the same TikZ drawings as in the PDF, converted to SVG. The notes are set in Charter, the face the printed notes use. No tracking, no cookies, no third-party requests.
